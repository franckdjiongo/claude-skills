## Naming Strategies

### A) Compound Words
Combine 2 short words that describe the function directly.
- Best for: Self-explanatory names
- Examples: TapSay, Dropbox, YouTube, WordPress
- Rule: Both words should be kindergarten-level vocabulary

### B) Prefix/Suffix Twists
Familiar word + creative modification.
- Best for: Unique-but-clear names
- Examples: Spotify (spot+ify), Shopify (shop+ify), Untype, Textly
- Patterns: Un-, Re-, -ify, -ly, -able, -ment

### C) Overlooked Real Words
Uncommon English words repurposed from a thesaurus.
- Best for: Premium/sleek brands
- Examples: Notion, Linear, Slack, Figma, Arc
- Rule: Must evoke the product without needing explanation
- WARNING: Common words (Cadence, Flow, Spark) are almost always taken

### D) Creative Mashups
Blend parts of recognizable words.
- Best for: Unique trademarkable names
- Examples: Pinterest (pin+interest), Instagram (instant+telegram), Talscribe (talk+transcribe)
- Rule: Source words must be recognizable in the mashup

### E) Action Verbs
What the product DOES, as a verb or gerund.
- Best for: Tool-oriented products
- Examples: Zoom, Splice, Fetch, Deploy
- Rule: Must be short and usable as a verb ("I'll just [name] it")

## Verification Criteria

### PASS Requirements (ALL must be true)
- No competing product in the same or adjacent category
- No major trademark holders in relevant Nice classes (especially Class 9: software, Class 42: SaaS)
- At least one good domain variant appears available (.com, .app, or get[name].com)
- Not a common word with 5+ existing branded products
- Easy to search for (not a generic term that returns unrelated results)

### CONDITIONAL Triggers
- Small company in unrelated field uses the name
- A parked/inactive domain exists but no active product
- Minor namespace noise (e.g., a Discord bot, a tiny GitHub project)
- Requires creative domain strategy (get-, try-, use- prefix)

### FAIL Triggers (ANY of these)
- Direct competitor uses the name (same product category)
- Major tech company holds trademark (>$1B market cap)
- Active product in adjacent category (same app stores, same audience)
- All obvious domain variants taken by active websites
- 5+ products already using the name (SEO nightmare)
- Recently acquired brand (even if dormant -- parent company holds IP)

## Common Failure Patterns

### Lessons learned from real branding sessions:

1. **Common English words are almost always taken.** Words like Cadence, Spark, Flow, Pulse, Echo, Trace -- these have dozens of products using them. Avoid unless verification proves otherwise.

2. **The voice/audio space is EXTREMELY crowded.** Words containing: voice, whisper, speech, talk, speak, dict-, flow, type, scribe, note, echo, sound -- all heavily branded.

3. **Romance language words are overused in tech.** Italian, French, Latin roots (Verbo, Parlo, Vox) -- many startups use these. Check thoroughly.

4. **Foreign language words need explanation = bad.** Users rejected Finnish words (Kirja, Kulma) because they weren't self-explanatory. If the name needs "it means X in Y language" to make sense, it will lose most people.

5. **Domain availability != website existence.** A domain can be registered (parked) without having an active website. Always check for actual active products, not just whether a domain resolves.

6. **App store search is critical.** Many products exist only on app stores without a web presence. Always search "[name] app store" and "[name] iOS/Android/Windows app."

7. **Compound words survive verification best.** Names like TapSay, Talscribe -- novel compounds of common words -- have the highest pass rate because they're new combinations.

8. **Generate 30+ candidates minimum.** Expect 70-85% failure rate from verification. You need volume to find winners.

## Domain Strategy

### Priority Order
1. `[name].com` -- Gold standard, always check first
2. `[name].app` -- Strong for software products, Google-managed TLD
3. `get[name].com` -- Standard startup pattern, always available-ish
4. `[name].io` -- Developer-friendly, good for tech products
5. `[name].dev` -- Google-managed, newer but credible
6. `try[name].com` -- Marketing-oriented alternative
7. `[name].so` -- Short, modern, less common

### Domain Red Flags
- `.ai` domains -- contradicts "no AI in name" constraint (if applicable)
- Hyphens in domains -- looks unprofessional
- Long domains (>15 chars) -- hard to type/remember
- Domains that spell differently than the product name

## Brand Vibe Archetypes

Use these to guide naming, slogans, and visual direction. The color direction is an
**open space of territories, not a default**. **It is forbidden to reuse an
archetype's first/default territory** — pick one of its alternatives (or derive a new
one) from a **specific product attribute** and write the justification. Visual craft
(typography pairings, palette rules, logo procedure, scoring) lives in
`references/visual-identity.md`.

### Professional & Sleek
- Reference brands: Notion, Linear, Stripe
- Naming style: Clean, single/compound words, premium feel
- Slogan tone: Confident, understated, precise
- Color territories (choose + justify from a product attribute; do not default):
  1. Deep indigo / navy base + teal accent
  2. Warm slate / graphite base + coral accent
  3. Ivory / bone base + deep forest accent
  4. Cool concrete gray base + burnt-amber accent
- Logo style: Geometric, minimal, monochrome-capable

### Playful & Modern
- Reference brands: Figma, Arc, Slack
- Naming style: Friendly, approachable, can be coined
- Slogan tone: Witty, energetic, conversational
- Color territories (choose + justify; ban lavender/purple per visual-identity.md):
  1. Sunset coral + warm cream
  2. Electric lime + charcoal
  3. Tangerine + soft ink navy
  4. Two-tone primary duo (e.g. teal + magenta) on off-white
- Logo style: Rounded, colorful, distinctive silhouette

### Techy & Powerful
- Reference brands: Warp, Raycast, Vercel
- Naming style: Short, punchy, action-oriented
- Slogan tone: Direct, no-nonsense, developer-friendly
- Color territories (dark-mode-first; choose + justify):
  1. Near-black base + electric cyan accent
  2. Deep space navy + acid-green accent
  3. Carbon base + signal-orange accent
  4. Monochrome black/white + a single high-chroma accent
- Logo style: Sharp, geometric, monospace-compatible

### Minimal & Elegant
- Reference brands: Apple, Nothing, Calm
- Naming style: Simple, one word, evocative
- Slogan tone: Quiet confidence, poetic, aspirational
- Color territories (muted; choose + justify):
  1. Warm off-white + graphite ink
  2. Sand / clay base + terracotta accent
  3. Pale sage base + deep evergreen accent
  4. Cool paper gray + single muted-blue accent
- Logo style: Ultra-thin, maximum simplicity, wordmark-focused
